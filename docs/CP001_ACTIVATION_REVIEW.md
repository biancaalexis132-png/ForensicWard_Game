## Compiled conversation

The following pairs are copied from the existing proposed five-encounter screenplay. Unpaired staging/Naomi lines and director notes remain in the full review source; they are not synthesized as patient dialogue. No line is clinically approved by this implementation.

### Establishing Trust

**Psychiatrist:** Hello, Jordan. I'm the psychiatrist following up today. What would be most useful for us to discuss?

**Jordan:** Sleep. And the way everyone asks if I take my medication as though that answers everything.

**Psychiatrist:** I hear that. Tell me about the nights you've been having.

**Jordan:** I fall asleep, then wake up. Some nights it's noise. Other nights I just lie there. I'm tired when the art group starts.

**Psychiatrist:** How has that affected the things you want to do?

**Jordan:** Sketching helps. I'm still trying to go. I just want enough energy to be there.

**Psychiatrist:** I also want to understand your mood, anxiety, and how you've been feeling overall, not only the sleep problem. Would it be alright to ask about those?

**Jordan:** Yes. Just tell me why you're asking instead of writing an answer before I give one.


### A Difficult Morning

**Psychiatrist:** I saw that you missed art group. Would you be comfortable telling me what happened?

**Jordan:** I slept badly. By the time I got up, it was almost finished. People keep saying I refused. I didn't refuse art.

**Psychiatrist:** So the attendance note doesn't capture your reason. I'd like to make sure your explanation is represented accurately.

**Jordan:** That's all I wanted.

**Psychiatrist:** How have your mood and worries been since we last spoke?

**Jordan:** I was worried people would think I was slipping backward. I know that's different from them actually saying it.

**Psychiatrist:** We can keep what you experienced, what someone observed, and what remains uncertain separate.

**Jordan:** That would be a start.


### The Treatment Conversation

**Psychiatrist:** Last time you wanted to understand what options you have. Is that still something you'd like to focus on?

**Jordan:** Yes. I know the chart says schizophrenia. I also get anxious about appointments, and sometimes I feel low. I don't want all of that put under one word.

**Psychiatrist:** We should look at those experiences individually and over time. I won't assume what causes them.

**Jordan:** Sometimes I worry that asking questions makes me seem difficult.

**Psychiatrist:** Asking questions is part of participating in your care. What do you most want to understand?

**Jordan:** What I'm taking, why, and whether anything could be contributing to the grogginess. I don't want changes made without an explanation.

**Psychiatrist:** That's reasonable to raise. The medication record still needs to be verified before we discuss any specific change.

**Jordan:** Thank you. Also, my sister Sam might know how things used to be. I need to think about whether I want you to contact her.


### A Setback, Not a Failure

**Psychiatrist:** One difficult night doesn't erase the work you've done. What felt different this time?

**Jordan:** It was quieter yesterday. Today I woke up worrying about the next review meeting. Then I couldn't fall back asleep.

**Psychiatrist:** We can record what you noticed, and consider the different contributors without guessing.

**Jordan:** I did finish a drawing this week. I wanted you to know that too.

**Psychiatrist:** That matters. It's part of how things are going, not separate from it.

**Jordan:** I don't need you to promise it'll be easy. I just need the plan to make sense when days like this happen.


### A Plan That Belongs to Jordan

**Psychiatrist:** We've met over several shifts now. Before I summarize, what feels most important to you?

**Jordan:** You ask what happened, not what you think should have happened. That makes it easier to tell the truth when a day goes badly.

**Psychiatrist:** You wanted better sleep, enough energy for art group, and a clearer understanding of treatment. Does that still capture your priorities?

**Jordan:** Yes. And I want someone to remember I'm more than the diagnosis.

**Psychiatrist:** I will record both what you reported and what we actually observed, plus what we haven't established yet.

**Jordan:** What if the next doctor starts the same questions again?

**Psychiatrist:** I'll prepare a handoff with your goals, the documented history, and any outstanding review items. That doesn't mean every question is answered.

**Jordan:** I'd rather have an honest plan than a perfect one on paper.
