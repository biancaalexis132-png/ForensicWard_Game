# Naomi v03: why v02 looked cartoonish, what v03 changes, what is still missing

**State (2026-10-18, Claude Code):** v03 is a visual-development candidate. It is **not approved** and **not production-ready**. It is the default only for the review switch (`-- --character-preview=staff_bennett`); normal play still shows the capsule. v01 and v02 files are untouched and still selectable (`staff_bennett:v02`, `staff_bennett:v01`).

Rebuild: `python -c "exec(open('tools/blender/characters/build_naomi_v03.py').read())"` with the `bpy` 5.2.2 module, or `blender -b -P tools/blender/characters/build_naomi_v03.py`. Settings are in `naomi_v03.json`; provenance is in `assets/asset_manifest/naomi_character_manifest_v03.json`.

## Diagnosis of v02

| Area | Cause in v02 | Share of the problem |
|---|---|---|
| Base mesh | Fine. The MPFB2 body under v02 is anatomically plausible. | Low |
| Helper geometry | MPFB's helper meshes (joint cubes, eye/teeth/tongue proxies, skirt and tights helpers) were exported unmasked. They produced the "skirt", the block between the feet and the oversized eyes. | **High** |
| Eyes and brows | Procedural spheres and strips added on top of the helpers. | High |
| Clothing | Scrubs were painted on the body and the skirt helper, so no separate trousers or hems. | High |
| Hair | Flat card strips. | High |
| Skin | One flat colour. | Medium |
| Rig | The armature sat 0.815 m below the mesh. Fine at rest, but any animation would tear the mesh. | Hidden, blocks animation |
| Pose | Bind T-pose. | Medium |
| Godot lighting | Not the cause. The same scene lights v03 acceptably. | Low |

## What v03 changes (all generated by the builder, from the v02 MPFB body)

- Helper geometry removed. The eyeballs are fitted to MPFB's own eye helpers, so they sit in the sockets at the right size.
- Rig realigned to the mesh, then a relaxed standing pose applied as rest (arms down, slight elbow bend).
- Baked 2K skin texture with tone variation, cheeks, lids, lash line, brows, lips and nails.
- Separate garments: fitted V-neck scrub top with short sleeves, two-leg straight trousers, closed practical shoes, a blank clip-on ID card (no invented name or logo).
- Pulled-back two-strand twists that taper at the hairline, gathered into a low bun, over a dark scalp cap.
- 44,692 triangles (v02: 32,281), one rig, 9 meshes.

Renders: `assets/renders/characters/Naomi_Bennett_v03_*.png`. In game: `ingame_review_v03/`.

## Still below production quality

1. **No animation and no facial blend shapes.** She holds one static pose; the acting cues still have nothing to drive.
2. **Skin** is procedural: no pores, no subsurface map, no specular breakup. It reads slightly plastic up close.
3. **Hair** is tube geometry: no flyaways, no strand-level detail, and the bun is simplified.
4. **Clothing** is a shell built from the body, not a simulated garment. There are no seams, pockets or folds, and the trouser hems ripple.
5. **Shoes** are lumpy at the toes.
6. **Face**: anatomy and eyes now read as a real adult, but brows are faint and there are no eyelashes, so the expression is a little blank.
7. **No LODs, and nothing is measured on the Mac.**

## Can the automated Blender/MPFB route reach believable realism?

It can reach a convincing **mid-distance NPC**, which is roughly what v03 is at the nursing-station counter. It cannot reach close-up realism on its own. The ceiling comes from three things a script cannot generate well: groomed hair, scanned or hand-painted skin detail, and simulated clothing.

**Recommended hybrid** (owner decision):
1. Keep the MPFB body and rig as the base. Licence: CC0 for the MakeHuman assets.
2. Use the MPFB/MakeHuman **CC0 asset packs** for skin, eyebrows, eyelashes and hair. These need the owner's Blender on the Mac with MPFB installed. The Blender add-on server was not running during this session, so they were not used here.
3. Finish with a manual pass: hair cards or a short groom for the twists, cloth simulation for the scrubs, then facial shape keys (visemes and blinks) and a short idle clip.
4. If the face still falls short, use a properly licensed realistic head base (for example a scan-based asset with a game-use licence) and fit it to the MPFB body. Record the licence in the manifest before importing anything.
