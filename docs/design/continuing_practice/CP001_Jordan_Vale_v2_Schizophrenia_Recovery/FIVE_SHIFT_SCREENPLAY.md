# CP-001 — Revised five-encounter screenplay: Schizophrenia and longitudinal recovery

> **PROPOSED DIALOGUE, NOT CLINICALLY APPROVED OR VOICED.** Jordan has an established *historical* schizophrenia diagnosis and potentially co-occurring mood/anxiety symptoms, without a currently confirmed comorbid syndrome. This is new fictional narrative material. Existing v1 dialogue is retained in the earlier package for comparison.

**Performance:** In everyday ward settings, Jordan is an individual with their own humor, priorities and boundaries. Do not encode psychosis as monstrous, uncanny or an environmental clue. No single choice determines access, competence, rapport, remission, relapse or the postgame institutional ending. Respect they/them pronouns. The patient can refuse/defer sensitive questions; the clinician may document `NOT_ASSESSED` and arrange clinically appropriate follow-up. Dialogue pacing and clinical actions require reviewer approval before shipping.

## Encounter 01 — Establishing Trust

**Where:** Nursing station → approved interview room. **Purpose:** Introduce the follow-up and invite Jordan to set priorities. Establish separately their present account, nursing reports and the historical chart diagnosis.

### Scene and performance
The ward continues ordinary work without horror music. Give Jordan conversational pauses and agency, with no fixed facial-expression shorthand for mental illness. The player approaches respectfully, with the option to review existing records before the encounter. No unapproved patient movement or locked-room crossing.

### Draft full conversation

**NAOMI (proposed):** Jordan asked whether today's visit would leave time to talk about sleep. The nursing notes are available; I don't want you to assume I know the cause.

**PLAYER:** Thank you. I will review the note and hear Jordan's account.

**PLAYER:** Hello, Jordan. I'm the psychiatrist following up today. What would be most useful for us to discuss?

**JORDAN:** Sleep. And the way everyone asks if I take my medication as though that answers everything.

**PLAYER:** I hear that. Tell me about the nights you've been having.

**JORDAN:** I fall asleep, then wake up. Some nights it's noise. Other nights I just lie there. I'm tired when the art group starts.

**PLAYER:** How has that affected the things you want to do?

**JORDAN:** Sketching helps. I'm still trying to go. I just want enough energy to be there.

**PLAYER:** I also want to understand your mood, anxiety, and how you've been feeling overall, not only the sleep problem. Would it be alright to ask about those?

**JORDAN:** Yes. Just tell me why you're asking instead of writing an answer before I give one.

**PLAYER:** Fair. Your chart lists schizophrenia, but I won't assume it explains everything you're describing.

### Meaningful player branches

**A — Lead with patient's goal.** Ask about sleep, function and preferred priorities; maintain shared agenda.
**B — Begin by comparing record sources.** Read the nursing summary first, explicitly separate it from what Jordan reports; then ask their perspective.
**C — Discuss historical diagnosis respectfully.** Explain that schizophrenia is listed in the chart; invite correction and concern without implying current symptoms.
**D — Jordan defers a topic.** Acknowledge the boundary, note `NOT_ASSESSED`, offer to revisit later. No penalty or hidden information gate.

### Documentable exit / state

Complete a source-attributed history note, including reported sleep and impact, and pending chart/medication verification. Do not automatically populate MSE, risk, anxiety/depression screen or psychotic symptom negatives. Unresolved medically relevant questions should be assigned for follow-up or supported escalation. Introduce the next optional encounter.

Dialogue skips and scene unloads must leave a valid resume point; the original patient report and nursing chart are not marked independently verified merely because they agree. ### Draft full conversation

**NAOMI:** Jordan wasn't at art group this morning. That is what the activity log says. I haven't drawn a conclusion about why.

**PLAYER:** Thanks. I'll review the entry and ask Jordan.

**PLAYER:** I saw that you missed art group. Would you be comfortable telling me what happened?

**JORDAN:** I slept badly. By the time I got up, it was almost finished. People keep saying I refused. I didn't refuse art.

**PLAYER:** So the attendance note doesn't capture your reason. I'd like to make sure your explanation is represented accurately.

**JORDAN:** That's all I wanted.

**PLAYER:** How have your mood and worries been since we last spoke?

**JORDAN:** I was worried people would think I was slipping backward. I know that's different from them actually saying it.

**PLAYER:** We can keep what you experienced, what someone observed, and what remains uncertain separate.

**C — Explore alternative contributors.** Ask about sleep, environmental disturbances, physical symptoms and the need for an approved medication review, without declaring a cause.
**D — Ask about worsening psychosis.** Offer a respectful, optional assessment prompt; any response must be explicitly authored and clinically reviewed, not improvised.

### Documentable exit / state

Document the observed attendance status and patient-explained fatigue separately. Update the symptom timeline and follow-up task. Do not auto-label relapse or risk; inability to access a record is a pending task, not a normal result.

## Encounter 03 — The Treatment Conversation

**Where:** Scheduled interview room. **Purpose:** Explore shared decision-making within the limits of unavailable medication and legal information.

### Scene and performance
The ward continues ordinary work without horror music. Give Jordan conversational pauses and agency, with no fixed facial-expression shorthand for mental illness. The player approaches respectfully, with the option to review existing records before the encounter. No unapproved patient movement or locked-room crossing.

### Draft full conversation

**PLAYER:** Last time you wanted to understand what options you have. Is that still something you'd like to focus on?

**JORDAN:** Yes. I know the chart says schizophrenia. I also get anxious about appointments, and sometimes I feel low. I don't want all of that put under one word.

**PLAYER:** We should look at those experiences individually and over time. I won't assume what causes them.

**JORDAN:** Sometimes I worry that asking questions makes me seem difficult.

**PLAYER:** Asking questions is part of participating in your care. What do you most want to understand?

**JORDAN:** What I'm taking, why, and whether anything could be contributing to the grogginess. I don't want changes made without an explanation.

**PLAYER:** That's reasonable to raise. The medication record still needs to be verified before we discuss any specific change.

**JORDAN:** Thank you. Also, my sister Sam might know how things used to be. I need to think about whether I want you to contact her.

**PLAYER:** We can leave that decision with you for now, and discuss what information would be shared before anything happens.

### Meaningful player branches

**A — Prioritize reconciliation.** Create a request for the authorized medication/MAR record; do not show speculative items.
**B — Map mood/anxiety symptoms.** Ask about onset, duration and interference using reviewed follow-up prompts; do not certify a comorbid diagnosis.
**C — Discuss possible collateral.** Ask whether Jordan authorizes contact and the limits. No call if consent not confirmed and authorization appropriate.
**D — Jordan declines collateral.** Respect decision; continue care with available information and document that sources are limited.

### Documentable exit / state

Preserve a source-separated preference note, unanswered questions and pending medication review. A treatment plan may be proposed for clinician review but no clinical order is committed, no unapproved treatment effect applied and no family information disclosed.

## Encounter 04 — A Setback, Not a Failure

**Where:** Optional follow-up after one or more shifts. **Purpose:** Reassess a difficult night in longitudinal context; protect patient agency and avoid the binary cured/relapsed model.

### Scene and performance
The ward continues ordinary work without horror music. Give Jordan conversational pauses and agency, with no fixed facial-expression shorthand for mental illness. The player approaches respectfully, with the option to review existing records before the encounter. No unapproved patient movement or locked-room crossing.

### Draft full conversation

**JORDAN:** I had another bad night. I thought we were getting somewhere, and now I feel like I'm back at the beginning.

**PLAYER:** One difficult night doesn't erase the work you've done. What felt different this time?

**JORDAN:** It was quieter yesterday. Today I woke up worrying about the next review meeting. Then I couldn't fall back asleep.

**PLAYER:** We can record what you noticed, and consider the different contributors without guessing.

**JORDAN:** I did finish a drawing this week. I wanted you to know that too.

**PLAYER:** That matters. It's part of how things are going, not separate from it.

**JORDAN:** I don't need you to promise it'll be easy. I just need the plan to make sense when days like this happen.

**PLAYER:** Let's identify what needs follow-up and who will be responsible, rather than treating this visit as a verdict.

### Meaningful player branches

**A — Review longitudinal pattern.** Compare patient-reported nights and functioning over time without inventing objective values.
**B — Ask about anxiety and mood.** Offer contextual, reviewed questions including relevant safety assessment as indicated; don't auto-generate safety conclusions.
**C — Celebrate a meaningful activity.** Acknowledge drawing as Jordan's stated goal without rewarding a superficial positivity choice.
**D — Handoff unresolved concern.** Identify recipient, communication event, status and next review; no false completed badge.

### Documentable exit / state

Update note and longitudinal timeline. Do not infer relapse, remission, medication failure, nonadherence, suicide risk or violence. Keep unresolved clinical issues visible after shift closure via an acknowledged handoff.

## Encounter 05 — A Plan That Belongs to Jordan

**Where:** Interview room → nursing station sign-out. **Purpose:** Review five encounters, explicitly report source limitations, record the patient's recovery priorities and provide a continuing-care handoff.

### Scene and performance
The ward continues ordinary work without horror music. Give Jordan conversational pauses and agency, with no fixed facial-expression shorthand for mental illness. The player approaches respectfully, with the option to review existing records before the encounter. No unapproved patient movement or locked-room crossing.

### Draft full conversation

**PLAYER:** We've met over several shifts now. Before I summarize, what feels most important to you?

**JORDAN:** You ask what happened, not what you think should have happened. That makes it easier to tell the truth when a day goes badly.

**PLAYER:** You wanted better sleep, enough energy for art group, and a clearer understanding of treatment. Does that still capture your priorities?

**JORDAN:** Yes. And I want someone to remember I'm more than the diagnosis.

**PLAYER:** I will record both what you reported and what we actually observed, plus what we haven't established yet.

**JORDAN:** What if the next doctor starts the same questions again?

**PLAYER:** I'll prepare a handoff with your goals, the documented history, and any outstanding review items. That doesn't mean every question is answered.

**JORDAN:** I'd rather have an honest plan than a perfect one on paper.

**NAOMI (proposed sign-out):** I'll review the outstanding items you handed off and document what has actually been acknowledged. The next team needs the open questions, not just the completed boxes.

**PLAYER:** Agreed. The handoff stays open until the right person receives responsibility.

### Meaningful player branches

**A — Patient-authored goal summary.** Jordan confirms or edits their priorities.
**B — Clinical uncertainty summary.** List pending medication, historical, medical and symptom details with original sources.
**C — Team handoff.** Communicate unresolved tasks with accountable role and acknowledgment; do not mark clinical resolution automatically.
**D — Continue ongoing relationship.** Close the authored five-encounter arc while leaving Jordan eligible for additional reviewed routine follow-ups; no forced discharge.

After signed note and safe handoff, mark `cp001_cycle_complete` once, but keep ongoing clinical care accessible. No recovery score, forced discharge, ending-specific case restriction, paranormal clue or treatment reward. Show a modest post-encounter reflection: Jordan's goals have become clearer even though symptoms and uncertainties persist.
