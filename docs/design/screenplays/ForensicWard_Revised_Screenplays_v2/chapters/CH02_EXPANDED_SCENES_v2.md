## Scene 01 — Back in the Routine (morning nursing station)
**Stage:** The ward remains actively staffed. A dietary cart squeaks past; an employee is dealing with transport scheduling. Bennett closes an overnight-report pane, not an occult evidence board.

**BENNETT:** Morning, Doctor. The W0-B access ticket is still with Information Security. That's the exciting news. The useful news is that we have patients to see.

**PLAYER:** Any significant changes overnight?

**BENNETT:** There are a few things to review. I'll show you the reports, but don't take my summary as your examination.

Player approaches: (A) review overnight clinical reports; (B) check outstanding patient-safety tasks; (C) ask which service owns the W0-B audit. All three remain possible; required clinical coverage cannot be bypassed.

**BENNETT:** If the computer turns out to be haunted, it's still going to expect us to finish charting.

**Exit condition:** relevant clinical work reviewed or assigned appropriately; audit lead remains a separate work item. No visual effect.

## Scene 02 — The Gap in the Log (authorized records review)
The records technician has two representations of an equipment-maintenance entry, with a four-minute discrepancy and a repeated minute in an export. The raw time zone, clock source and sort order are unknown until verified.

**TECHNICIAN:** The export displays local time without the source offset. The original console doesn't. That's one perfectly ordinary way for two reports to disagree.

**PLAYER:** Can we preserve the originals before anybody fixes a display problem?

**TECHNICIAN:** Already requested. And there's one repeated minute in this export I haven't accounted for yet.

Choices: compare documented clock sources; request authorized source-system export; record unresolved until reconciliation. Player never accesses restricted records merely by clicking an option.

**Evidence state:** reported discrepancy; NOT proven loss of time. No paranormal label.

## Scene 03 — Mara Ellis: A Patient, Not a Clue
**Established dossier (provisional record):** age 37, former records specialist, concerns involving aunt Lillian Ellis's Oct 12 / Oct 14, 1988 records; fatigue with reported 4–5 hours sleep, anxiety, repeated checking; historical bipolar-spectrum label not adequately established; documented organized speech/thought during evaluation; sister Dana is potential collateral, subject to consent and applicable authority. Historical passive death wishes reported; current SI/HI denial is by report, not a guarantee of safety. Medication history requires reconciliation. The exact legal question must be checked against the actual order.

**Setting:** Bright, neutral clinical interview room. Mara is neat, guarded, and holding a folder. No eerie music or sensational costume.

**PLAYER:** I'm Dr. [custom name], one of the psychiatrists here. I'd like to understand how you're doing, and why you've been referred.

**MARA:** Are you here to examine me or tell me I'm wrong?

Opening alternatives, all professionally defensible:
- A: **Role clarity.** PLAYER: I'm here to assess your health and hear your account. The records question can be documented separately. MARA: Then please actually look at what I brought.
- B: **Acknowledge frustration.** PLAYER: It sounds as though you've had to repeat yourself. Where would you like to begin? MARA: With the pages people keep dismissing.
- C: **Explain limits.** PLAYER: My role is clinical care. I won't promise an administrative finding, but I can document your concerns accurately. MARA: That's more than most people have told me.

Mara places two copies on the table.

**MARA:** October twelfth. October fourteenth. Same person. My aunt, Lillian. Different dates.

**PLAYER:** Where did each copy originate?

**MARA:** One was a paper record, the other a later archive copy. I worked in records. I know mistakes happen. I also know a question is different from an accusation.

**PLAYER:** What do you think could explain it?

**MARA:** I don't know. It might be an error. I want somebody to check instead of deciding what kind of person I am for asking.

### Interview modules — can be taken in flexible order
**Sleep and function:**
PLAYER: How much sleep are you getting? How do you feel the next day?
MARA: Four or five hours. I'm exhausted. I try to stop checking the documents, then I think of something else I missed.
**Documentation:** Reduced sleep with fatigue; does NOT demonstrate decreased need for sleep or mania.

**Longitudinal mood history:**
PLAYER: An earlier note mentions a mood disorder. Can we discuss what was happening during that period?
MARA: I've had difficult periods. I'm not saying I'm never unwell. I'm saying the pages still say what they say.
**Documentation:** Previous labels require dates, symptom duration, functional change, prior notes and collateral; no definitive bipolar or psychotic diagnosis established by this exchange.

**Belief and alternatives:**
PLAYER: What other explanations have you considered for the date conflict?
MARA: Copying mistakes. Bad indexing. Someone might have entered something later. I don't know which.
**Documentation:** Acknowledges alternatives; do not substitute perceived implausibility for a delusion assessment.

**Safety:**
PLAYER: When the stress feels overwhelming, have you thought about suicide, hurting yourself or somebody else?
MARA: I've felt hopeless in the past. I'm not thinking about hurting myself now.
**Documentation:** Current response is patient report; full risk formulation, protective factors and prior history remain necessary. Do not infer HI response or other negatives not explicitly assessed.

**Collateral:**
PLAYER: Who might know how your sleep and daily routine have changed? We can discuss what information might be requested and shared.
MARA: My sister, Dana. I don't want her to think I'm being accused of anything.
PLAYER: We can clarify the purpose and applicable consent before contacting her.
**Existing collateral source:** Dana reports increased late-night checking, worry and fatigue; older increased activity remains insufficiently characterized. Distinguish Dana's account from clinician observations and security descriptions.

**End:**
MARA: If you confirm the dates differ, will that change how you see me?
PLAYER: It would establish something about the records. Your clinical assessment still depends on your history, examination and reliable information.
MARA: I can live with that. Please keep those separate.

**Player handoff:** sleep/functional support and appropriate safety follow-up, legal-role clarification, medication reconciliation; separate request to authorized Records office. No forced medicine, diagnosis or forensic opinion.

## Scene 04 — Institutional Friction: Voss
Voss asks the protagonist to meet privately. Staffing and quality reports on her desk reflect a busy, functioning institution.

**VOSS:** An incomplete entry can become an allegation very quickly. I'm not asking you to ignore it. I'm asking you to tell me what was actually established.
**PLAYER:** An inactive credential appears in an archived transfer-request audit. It doesn't identify the person who used it.
**VOSS:** Good. Document patient-safety concerns separately from information-security concerns. They have different owners.

Branches: request written process; advocate urgent safety assessment when supported; propose independent records reconciliation. Voss may disagree about procedure, but does not falsify records or become a default villain.

## Scene 05 — A Working Explanation
An analyst tests clock skew, migrated location codes, export sorting and identifier collision. Each may explain part of the discrepancy; at least one open item remains.

**ANALYST:** The familiar explanations aren't glamorous. They're also where we have to start.

**ANALYST:** We preserve the mismatch and keep checking.

Choices: source extraction, identifier crosswalk, request neutral second review. Do not elevate contradictory timestamps to proven altered time.

## Scene 07 — An Incomplete Witness
A Facilities worker recalls an elevator bell during a scheduled shutdown.

**WORKER:** I heard the old lift bell. Could've been the test cycle. Sound carries strangely through the pipes.
**PLAYER:** Did you actually see anyone use the elevator?
**WORKER:** No. I wouldn't say I did.

Choices: confirm work orders, capture recollection as reported, verify timing without leading questions. The statement is witness memory, not direct observation of movement.

## Scene 08 — The Door That Wasn't Left Open

**ESCORT:** Someone could have used the maintenance route.

## Scene 09 — A File for the Wrong Day

## Scene 10 — Missing Hours (closing beat)
The analyst can account for some of the timing mismatch but not the entire reported interval.

**ANALYST:** I can show you where the clocks differ. I can't reconstruct this interval responsibly from these records alone.
**PLAYER:** Then the report should say that.

The player returns to the staff entrance. Bennett is ending her shift.

**BENNETT:** Any luck?
**PLAYER:** We confirmed there are records we can't reconcile yet.
**BENNETT:** Then you're doing what you're supposed to. Don't decide the answer first.

An optional personal-contact message arrives, keyed to the player's moderate personal history. No patient information is disclosed. Fade out over an ordinary hospital exterior.

**Chapter evidence boundary:** only audit inconsistencies, source disagreement, an unobserved door movement and uncertain witness recollection. No confirmed supernatural event. Mara is not recruited to investigate Ward Zero.
